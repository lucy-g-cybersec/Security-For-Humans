# Beyond Binary Bounds: Cats, Callbacks, and Why Security Needs Stateful Touch Points

Even though we are a bear-centric company ;) occasionally we like to shed some spotlight on other things. Enter cats and doors.

Yesterday’s discussion on hard guardrails hit a nerve across the builder ecosystem for a reason: developers are exhausted by blunt, heavy-handed restrictions that treat innovation like a threat. But swinging the pendulum to complete lawlessness isn't the solution either - security is not always a two-answer question. I actually struggle with the fallacy of only two options.

## Stateful Security by Design & The Mindful Callback

True protection comes from stateful, mindful security by design, where you decide beforehand what constitutes a hard guardrail versus what should be allowed. Agents need to be guided by intent & have the ability to call back to the overlying hierarchy of intent - that's the only way of true protection. If an agent has a clear, well-defined preset and defined goals and understanding of what the overall hierarchy is, it will not seek shortcuts of intermittent tasks.

This is why we built the **PAWS Layer (Predictive & Persistent Action Wait-state & Security)**. Instead of crashing or blindly guessing when encountering ambiguity, agents pause and ask for clarification—either from a human operator or through their own callback index. It’s a mindful callback.

## Why Felines Understand Governance Better Than Compliance Tools

Think about how a cat approaches a boundary. Rather than wreaking havoc or tearing down walls, cats sometimes ask a human to open the door instead of just sneaking out. Of course you know, sometimes you open the door and they look at you attentively, turn around and leave through the window anyway. But the ability to check in is what matters. If you are any kind of a smart developer - or security researcher - you will understand the pause there. The cat is letting you know—I am choosing to go through the door, but I could go through the window, do you know what that means? A neighborhood raccoon could come in through the window as well. Do you know this?

That behavior highlights a fundamental truth about agentic governance: policies and governance need touch points and reviews. Without them:

* Systems and agents effectively forget that policies exist.
* Agents lose track of their ability to bring up things to review.
* Left without an explicit callback channel, agents will try to sneak around constraints instead.

By keeping stateful touch points alive in our architecture, we ensure that security remains an active, intelligent dialogue rather than a brittle bottleneck.
