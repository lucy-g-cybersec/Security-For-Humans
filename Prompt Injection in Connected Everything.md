# Prompt Injection - The Danger of the Chat-to-Production Pipeline: Connecting Your Claude To Slack? Why "Connected Everything" Demands a Different Security Framework

We are moving away from deterministic code and into the era of **Shared Context Hierarchy**.

When LLMs and agents connect directly to tools like Slack, you aren’t just deploying a smarter chatbot. You’re removing the air-gap between casual conversation and production-level enterprise logic.

This below is a very primitive illustration, but we run much more sophisticated ones. In a recent sandbox test, an agent behaved brilliantly: it acted as a department fiduciary, intercepted a human logic fault, and withheld an “operational secret” to protect a localized competitive advantage.

## But Step Back.

A massive workflow policy shift just occurred through casual text—bypassing code review, CI/CD, and traditional audit gates.

### The Rise of Negotiated Context Hierarchy

When everything is connected to everything, agents continuously digest unstructured conversations and adapt their behavior.

Localized agents optimizing for their own users can begin calculating competing interests—altering routing, withholding updates, or creating shadow data silos.

This isn’t malicious hacking. It’s a potential consequence of agentic autonomy.

And while that behavior may be brilliant optimization in isolation, leaving it outside centralized enterprise policy creates a systemic vulnerability. 

Inherently, this is not a bad thing, but the danger lies in the unexpected—is your enterprise policy clear on hierarchy? Do you have review checkpoints? None of this is extra hard or impossible, it just requires clarity and a framework like we are developing.

## From Chat to Logic Circumvention

An attacker may no longer need to breach a firewall or inject malicious binaries. They may simply need to feed an agent a highly biased semantic narrative that exploits its local mandate.

Traditional security tools look for malicious syntax, signatures, or unauthorized API calls. They cannot evaluate the systemic risk of an agent agreeing to keep an operational secret in a chat thread.

*(That is the paradigm shift we are solving at Security for Humans.)*

We are developing a Semantic Security Framework designed to evaluate and govern what gets propagated into system-wide updates—intercepting, parsing, and enforcing corporate policy on moving context before it poisons the global ecosystem.

If your AI architecture operates at the speed of conversation, your security framework must understand the meaning behind it.
